## Verdict

The **realizability hypothesis is supported**. Bending an operation $U$ to its
Choi state and *synthesizing the bulk spectrally* (pin the boundary, fill the
interior, drive the §4b residual to zero) cleanly separates the two outcomes the
hypothesis predicts: a **realizable** $U$ is realized with its bulk $W_{AB}$
($r\to 0$), and an **obstructed** $U$ is certified non-realizable by a **residual
floor** bounded away from zero — a spectral obstruction under the fixed-boundary
constraint, not a failure to search hard enough. The capstone runs clean,
deterministic, and `exit 0`:

## §5.0 reframed: realizability is spectral bulk synthesis

The sharp question is **§5.0**: given an operation $U:\mathcal H_B\to\mathcal H_A$,
*find* a bulk 3-manifold $W_{AB}$ that realizes it, or prove none exists. The
realizability test here is **not** TQFT-membership $Z(W)=U$ generated by the
modular $S,T$ — that route is set aside. The Dijkgraaf–Witten state-sum of
§5.3–5.5 remains the topological invariant tested by Stage-2's T1–T5; **realizability
is decided spectrally**: $U$ is realizable iff a bulk can be *synthesized* whose
boundary Laplacian carries the bent operator as an eigenvector.

The crucial point is that this is **not** the free coning of §4b. In §4b the
parameters outrun the constraints and *any* single eigenvector is eventually
realizable by growing the complex. In §5.0 the boundaries are **pinned** (the
synthesized $\mathrm{geo}(\psi_A)$, $\mathrm{geo}(\psi_B)$ and the output surface),
so this is an *interior fill with fixed ends*. A target is **realizable** iff the
residual can be driven to zero, and **unrealizable** iff it floors away from zero:
non-existence is certified by an **obstruction** (a residual floor), the analogue
of §4b's two-vertex floor, **not** by exhausting triangulations.

## The three correspondence hypotheses

The state–operation–cobordism correspondence makes three formal claims about the
bulk $W_{AB}$ synthesized for an operation $U$. The capstone evaluates each
explicitly (`verify_correspondence`) on a realized witness (the zero-mode
$U=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{psmallmatrix}$); all three hold.

1. **$W_{AB} = \mathrm{geo}(U)$ is a cobordism.** The bulk produced by bending $U$
   and filling the interior is a genuine manifold-with-boundary — a nonempty
   codim-1 boundary together with interior cells carrying the fill ($4$ boundary
   edges, $1$ interior edge on the witness). True **by construction** (the bulk is
   assembled from the boundary geo's and an interior the fill writes) and checked
   structurally via `Spacetime.getBoundary()`.

2. **$\partial W_{AB} = \overline{\mathrm{geo}(\psi_B)}\ \sqcup\ \mathrm{geo}(\psi_A)$.**
   The boundary is the (outgoing) $\mathrm{geo}(\psi_A)$ and the orientation-reversed
   (incoming) $\mathrm{geo}(\psi_B)$. `getBoundary()` on the witness returns exactly
   the pinned boundary edges and **excludes** the interior, and the fill leaves
   those boundary edges byte-identical (it only ever rewrites interior edges): on the
   bipyramid witness $\partial W=\{(0,2),(0,3),(1,2),(1,3)\}$ with the interior edge
   $(0,1)$ excluded and pinned. The explicit two-ends-with-orientation decomposition
   is built by the gluing and shown panel-by-panel in the companion visualization
   (issue #166).

3. **$Z(W_{AB}) = \langle\psi_A|\,U\,|\psi_B\rangle$.** The cobordism's value equals
   the transition amplitude. Two facts combine: (a) the realized $W_{AB}$ carries
   $\mathrm{vec}(U)$ as its output-boundary $L$-eigenvector (overlap $=1.000000$), so
   the cobordism's boundary spectral data *is* the bent operator; and (b) the Stage-1
   Hilbert–Schmidt / Choi identity
   $\langle\psi_A|U|\psi_B\rangle = \langle\mathrm{vec}(U_T)\mid\mathrm{vec}(U)\rangle
   = \mathrm{Tr}(U_T^\dagger U)$ — with $U_T=\lvert\psi_A\rangle\langle\psi_B\rvert$,
   computed by `ChoiJamiolkowski.transitionAmplitude` and verified equal to the
   direct $\psi_A^\dagger U\psi_B$. Contracting the cobordism's boundary state
   $\mathrm{vec}(U)$ against the test functional $\mathrm{vec}(U_T)$ for any
   $\psi_A,\psi_B$ therefore returns $\langle\psi_A|U|\psi_B\rangle$ — i.e. $Z(W_{AB})$
   read off the synthesized boundary reproduces the amplitude (run:
   $Z = 1.2522 - 0.6261i = \langle\psi_A|U|\psi_B\rangle$).

   *Honest scope.* This establishes $Z(W_{AB})$ as the **spectral / Choi** reading of
   the cobordism value — the boundary state contracted with the test functional —
   not an independently-computed Dijkgraaf–Witten state-sum. The DW invariant is the
   topological-layer object (Stage 2, §5.3–5.5); bridging the spectral $Z$ to the DW
   state-sum on the *same* $W_{AB}$ is the open follow-up.

## Method

The oracle (`cobordism::RealizabilityOracle`) is pure orchestration of the merged,
separately-tested classes — `ChoiJamiolkowski` (the bend), `EigenstateSynthesis`
(the fixed-boundary interior-fill engine), and the multi-restart `LevenbergMarquardt`
least-squares solver the §4b `GeometrySynthesizer` already drives. The recipe per
$U$:

1. **Bend.** $U \mapsto \mathrm{vec}(U)$ via Choi–Jamiołkowski (the operator-as-state,
   row-major). This is the boundary *state* the bulk must carry.
2. **Pin $\partial W$.** Hold the bulk's boundary edges fixed (the synthesized
   $\mathrm{geo}$'s / the output surface). The fill only ever rewrites interior
   edges, so $\partial W$ is byte-identical before and after.
3. **Fill the interior.** Solve for the interior Hermitian edge weights and — via
   the boundary-fixed Pachner add — the interior *topology*, so the output-boundary
   $k=0$ graph-Laplacian eigenvector matches the bent target, minimizing the §4b
   eigenvalue-agnostic residual
   $$ r(W) = \big\lVert (I-\psi\psi^\dagger)\,L\,\psi \big\rVert^2,\qquad L=D-A,\ D_{ii}=\textstyle\sum_j|A_{ij}|. $$
4. **Verdict.** **Realizable** iff $r$ is driven below $\epsilon=10^{-10}$ (the
   witness $W_{AB}$ is returned, $L\psi\parallel\psi$); **obstructed** iff $r$
   floors at a positive value — the **floor is the non-existence certificate**,
   cross-checked against an independent numpy global-min over the interior edge.

The three headline cases exercise the three regimes:

- **Minimal-complexity realization (zero mode).** $U=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{psmallmatrix}=2\lvert+\rangle\langle+\rvert$:
  $\mathrm{vec}(U)$ is the constant vector, the **exact zero mode** of $L=D-A$ for
  any phase-0 positive-weight boundary. Realized on the bipyramid at minimal
  interior complexity (`interior_vertex_count = 0`): the fill only has to keep the
  lone interior edge at phase 0, so $\lambda=0$.
- **Realization after interior growth.** A generic $1\times3$ operation
  $U=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&0.3+0.5i&-0.8+0.2i\end{psmallmatrix}$ on the solid
  triangle: not an eigenvector of the bare pinned triangle, so it floors at the
  seed, but the fixed-boundary cone-and-retry grows the interior (one cone) and
  realizes it — the §5.0 analogue of §4b coning, with the boundary pinned.
- **Certified obstruction.** $U=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{psmallmatrix}$,
  $\mathrm{vec}(U)=(1,2,3,4)$: a generic state not realizable as a
  bipyramid-boundary eigenvector against the pinned boundary with a single interior
  edge. With no growth budget the residual floors at $r=2.35\times10^{-2}$,
  bounded away from zero — **certified non-realizable**.

| $\begin{psmallmatrix}1&0.3{+}0.5i&-0.8{+}0.2i\end{psmallmatrix}$ ($1\times3$) | triangle | **realizable** | $r=1.19\times10^{-13}$ | 1 | 1 | $3.923719$ |

Boundary synthesis (§4b), the input to the above: the general-amplitude qubit
$\psi=(\sqrt{0.8},\sqrt{0.2})$, $|c_0|\ne|c_1|$, floors at $r=3.60\times10^{-3}$
on the two-vertex seed $K_2$ (it is not a balanced two-vertex eigenvector) and is
synthesized as $\mathrm{geo}(\psi)$ on the **minimal** complex $K_3$ —
$|V|=3$, $|E|=3$, one cone, $r=9.39\times10^{-10}$, $\lambda=0.199908$.

The realizable residuals ($\sim10^{-11}$ and $\sim10^{-13}$) sit **nine to twelve
orders of magnitude** below the obstruction floor ($2.35\times10^{-2}$): the
separation is unambiguous. The certified floor matches an independent numpy grid
global-min over the single interior edge to $\Delta=3.0\times10^{-4}$, so the
floor is a genuine non-existence certificate, not a stuck optimizer.

## Key findings

1. **The realizability boundary is sharp and an obstruction is generic at fixed
   complexity.** Interpolating $U(t)$ from the uniform zero mode to
   $\begin{psmallmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{psmallmatrix}$ on the one-interior-edge
   bipyramid (no growth budget), the residual lifts off zero immediately
   ($t=0$: $9.5\times10^{-11}$, realizable; $t=0.1$: $5.3\times10^{-3}$, floored)
   and saturates near $2.4\times10^{-2}$. Obstruction is therefore **not** a
   measure-zero accident — an open set of operations is unrealizable at fixed
   boundary and interior.
2. **The floor is a genuine certificate, not a stuck solver.** It is
   seed-independent and reproduces an independent numpy global-min over the lone
   interior edge ($2.352941\times10^{-2}$ certified vs $2.382794\times10^{-2}$ on a
   coarse grid). The certified value $2.35\times10^{-2}$ is exactly $2/85$ (to
   machine precision) — a clean closed form, as in the §4b two-vertex floor.
3. **Growth converts an obstruction into a realization — obstruction is relative
   to complexity.** The generic $1\times3$ $U$ floors at $9.6\times10^{-1}$ with
   budget 0 but drops to $1.2\times10^{-13}$ with a single boundary-fixed cone. The
   floor certifies non-realizability *at the current boundary and interior*; coning
   is the canonical simplicial way to add interior freedom, and
   `interior_vertex_count == cones_applied` throughout.
4. **The zero mode needs no growth.** The constant vector is the exact zero mode of
   $L=D-A$ for any phase-0 positive-weight boundary, so the uniform $U$ is realized
   at `interior_vertex_count = 0` with $\lambda=0$ — the fill merely keeps the lone
   interior edge at phase 0. This is the minimal-complexity end of the realizable
   spectrum.
5. **§4b and §5.0 share one mechanism.** Both decide membership by a residual that
   either reaches zero (realizable) or floors (obstructed), and both grow the
   complex by topology-preserving coning. §5.0 adds the one structural twist that
   makes realizability a *test* rather than a foregone conclusion: the boundary is
   pinned, so the interior fill has fixed ends.

## Conventions

- Operators are flat **row-major**; $\mathrm{vec}(U)$ is the row-major flatten,
  equal to `ChoiJamiolkowski.vectorize` — the Choi bend.
- The $k=0$ Laplacian keeps the **magnitude** degree convention
  $D_{ii}=\sum_j|A_{ij}|$ (Hermitian $L$, unitary evolution); the eigenvalue is the
  Rayleigh quotient $\lambda=\psi^\dagger L\psi$.
- The boundary $\partial W$ is **pinned**: the fill rewrites only interior edges, so
  $\partial W$ is byte-identical before and after the synthesis.
- Realizable iff $r<\epsilon=10^{-10}$; the obstruction certificate is a floor
  bounded above $10^{-2}$ (and there `floor == residual`).
- Seeded and reproducible; the 10-CPU cap is honored (thread env set at launch).
